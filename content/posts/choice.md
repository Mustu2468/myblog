Humans, in their obsession with justifying their 'poignant' existence, attach meaning to anything and everything, and tend, almost always, to consume themselves with futile arguments to give their meaningless life a sense of 'purpose' to strive towards. One such argument I have stumbled upon many times is whether we actually have a “choice” in our lives. 

Here, by “choice”, I mean not the trivial decisions we make while going through the day, proudly owning that they’re ours, when they’re dictated by the capitalist, consumerist economy we live in, giving us the illusion of choice when we actually don’t have it. We are just pawns; manipulated and exploited.

By “choice”, I also do not mean the ethical and moral 'compass' we construct for ourselves to navigate, based on what we have been fed throughout our lives, which helps our conscience grapple with the guilt that follows our actions. Actions that are merely the coordinates fetched from the compass that we’ve built. Built either in obedience or in defiance of societal acceptance. Just repercussions from the biases. 

To clarify, one may argue here that the ‘compass’ is built upon an individual's choices: what he observes and what he seems to think is ‘right’ and ‘wrong’ are based on his own choices. They forget that these choices of theirs are nothing but a by-product of the environment they’re raised in. For exceptions to occur in this case, triggers must be present in the environment, triggers that would either make them defy or obey the conventional ethics and morality set to direct the masses.

Yes, by that rationale, existence can shelter under the umbrella of 'correlation and causality', which is a possible conclusion for our observable, foreseeable reality. But where is the humanity in that?

By *choice*, I mean the choice to just, simply exist. To want to sleep, lift your hand, see through your eyes, and *breathe*. The conscious effort that we actively make every second to continue living. As Prince Hamlet sorrowfully remarked, “To be, or not to be". A thought so vastly covered and debated upon since time immemorial. Countless philosophers, scientists, poets, writers, rationalists, and other fellow common humans have devoted their lives’ essence to answering this utterly unanswerable yet profound question, for it hides the subtlest of truisms.

As it ultimately doesn't matter whether we have a choice or not, our primal burden as living organisms remains to live and survive until our last breath. To keep defying what is expected of our intelligence to react, upon realising the sheer scale of our impotence, as our belief in our perception determines our 'will to live'.

Let entropy define the randomness of the incomprehensible universe, of which our existence may be a negligible probability, yet here we are, a probability nonetheless. Let everything be mind-bendingly statistical and utter chaos, yet here we are, a fragile melody.

Let any God be the judge, and our life be a test of pain and frivolity, yet here we are, standing proud as subjects. Let the cosmos be the silent absurd, ignorant of our cries, yet here we are, accepting the deafening silence of it all. For our existence matters to us and only us, and that is *enough*.
