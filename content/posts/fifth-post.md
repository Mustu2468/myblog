When I told the food vendor that the price of vegetables was *brazenly unrealistic* (trying to imitate my mother’s exceptional art of bargaining), she shouted at me, “Haven’t you ever bought vegetables?!” To which my over-analysing brain pondered, “I have! Just not in the traditional landscape, as I have been *cultured* in technology through modern capitalism, getting all that I could possibly need with a ‘click’ and a ‘clack’.” But, in reality, I was only able to mutter, “Sorry, aunty. Your price sounds good.”  

Behold, I conquered the quest to buy vegetables by confronting a real person. My grin could be seen a kilometre away, as I saw the grocery bag being filled to the brim with potatoes, tomatoes, onions, ginger, and carrots. Then, out of nowhere, she also stuffed some ‘કોથમીર’, which I hadn’t asked for. Pouncing on my fear of incapacity to tolerate sudden jolts in an ordained deal, I struck her with, ”Aunty-ji! Why are you giving me ‘કોથમીર’? I didn’t want it.” She smirked and replied, “It’s complimentary, son. Don’t worry, it’s my token of gratitude.” Unable to digest her generosity, I thought that there must be an ulterior motive behind this strategic move of hers.

Upon returning home, I questioned my mother, bewildered by the vendor’s gesture, about why she grabbed me free ‘કોથમીર’. And learned that it is a tradition, in order to establish simple goodwill, lost to the ever-demanding consumerism these days. My mother later used it to garnish the ‘ઢોકળાં’ she had made the next evening. 

If only ‘કોથમીર’ had thoughts and emotions, what would they be? To see itself wither away in the already short life it has, only to be assigned no value for the texture and mild sweetness it gives to the dishes, in this value-obsessed world, how would it justify the ‘purpose’ of its life? What would it feel when watching ‘salt’ and ‘sugar’ get all the fame and publicity through poets and chefs combined, for how *they* are the ‘underdogs’, whereas, *it* is just another spice? Or would it just accept the ‘absurd’ and triumphantly live whatever the circumstances are, till its last breath?

Huh! Funny how life encapsulates itself in allegories.
